# Election data discrepancies

The Ballotics JSON and CSV remain the research source of record. The packages retain their values and attach reviewed comparisons with clear source labels. The lab uses a separate unresolved fill for verified state-count discrepancies and selected attribution conflicts. Results and source files remain available for inspection.

| Case | Interpretation |
| --- | --- |
| 1788–1800 | Electors could cast two undifferentiated votes each. A state CSV sum near half the national sum is expected. Maryland’s 1796 count is nevertheless one above the Archives appointment count. In 1800 the national rows omit John Jay’s one vote. Jefferson won the House vote after tying Burr, 73–73. |
| 1808, 1816 and 1820 | The Archives distinguish appointed electors from ballots cast. Kentucky had an uncast vote in 1808, Delaware and Maryland had four uncast votes in 1816, and three electors died before voting in 1820. Source state and majority differences need separate review. |
| 1824 and 1836 | The House chose Adams in 1824 after Jackson led in electoral votes. Source state rows give South Carolina nine votes instead of eleven. In 1836 the Senate selected the **vice president**. The source state rows add four votes to Illinois. |
| 1848 and 1872 | In 1848 the Ballotics reported total of 296 exceeds the 290 counted by six. In 1872 the CSV includes votes for Arkansas and Louisiana that the Archives table does not count. Three Georgia votes for deceased candidate Greeley were rejected. The source groups several other named recipients and reports a majority of 176. The Archives report 177. |
| 1960 | Alabama’s 5–6 Kennedy/Byrd split, eight Mississippi votes for Byrd and one Oklahoma vote for Byrd are legitimate. The source Maine and Oregon counts differ by one each in opposite directions. |
| 1972, 1976, 1988 and 2004 | The state/count comparison locates missing named faithless votes including John Hospers in Virginia, Ronald Reagan in Washington, Lloyd Bentsen in West Virginia and John Edwards in Minnesota. The 1988 Missouri difference is separate. |
| 2000 and 2016 | One of D.C.’s appointed electors abstained in 2000, so the state row represents three appointed electors while the Archives show two Gore votes. In 2016 the national source groups the seven faithless votes. Texas omits two in its state sum, while Hawaii and Washington combine them into the main candidate counts. |
| 2024 | The sources all sum to 538, but thirteen state rows differ from the official apportionment. The published party totals from the national source remain distinct from the sum of state rows by recipient. |

The 151 internal count mismatches retain both quantities and all supplied candidate counts. Two additional rows have an aggregate in contention for the lead. Alaska’s 2020 federal overseas row reconciles internally at 433, but the official return shows 438 including five write-ins. Its normalized result status is unresolved. Source ranks and shares remain available, and disagreements suppress normalized shares and margins if they invalidate the denominator. [Official Alaska return](https://www.elections.alaska.gov/results/20GENR/data/sovc/HD99p.pdf).
